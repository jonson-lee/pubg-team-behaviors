# Which In-Game Behaviors Should a PUBG Team Train First?

## Statistical Analysis of Team Behaviors and Final Placement in Squad-FPP Matches

**Course:** IBA6101 - Statistical Analysis  
**Project status:** Go  
**Prepared:** 12 September 2026  
**Team:** Group project team (member names and student IDs omitted from this public repository).

## Executive summary

PUBG team coaches must decide how to allocate scarce training time across combat, mobility, resource management, and coordination. This proposal studies which team-level behaviors are most stably associated with final placement in standard Squad-FPP matches. The main output will be an uncertainty-aware training-priority ranking, not a causal claim or a training return-on-investment estimate.

The project uses the Kaggle PUBG Finish Placement Prediction data. Player records will be aggregated to one team in one match (`matchId + groupId`), because teammates share the same final placement. The primary outcome is continuous `winPlacePerc`. We will use descriptive statistics, pre-specified block hypotheses, multiple linear regression, confidence intervals, residual diagnostics, and match-level validation. Duo-FPP and Solo-FPP will be supplementary checks; special modes will be excluded.

## 1. Business background and problem

In a competitive team environment, a coach cannot give equal training attention to every possible behavior. A useful analytics question is therefore not simply “which players have better statistics?” but:

> Which controllable team behavior categories should receive training priority, given their observed association with final placement and the uncertainty of the evidence?

The data are observational and mostly record what happened during a completed match. The project is consequently a post-match training diagnostic. It will not claim that changing a behavior will cause a higher placement, and it will not calculate training ROI.

## 2. Objective and research questions

### Objective

Estimate the association between team-level in-game behavior and final placement percentile, test whether behavior blocks add explanatory power beyond match context, and translate stable results into a defensible training-priority ranking.

### Research questions

1. What are the distributions of final placement and team behaviors in standard Squad-FPP matches?
2. Do combat, mobility/resources, and team-coordination blocks provide incremental explanatory power after context controls?
3. Are the estimated relationships stable across model specifications and match-level resampling?
4. Do the main relationships appear in Duo-FPP and Solo-FPP comparison samples?
5. What can a coach reasonably prioritize without confusing association with causation?

## 3. Data source and feasibility

The main source is the Kaggle PUBG Finish Placement Prediction dataset, archived locally under `data/raw/pubg/` with its source card in `data/meta/pubg_source.md`. The source card records approximately 4.45 million training lines and 1.93 million test lines, field definitions, hashes, and known leakage risks.

The initial audit found approximately 1.76 million `squad-fpp` player rows across 18,576 matches, yielding about 506,161 team-match observations after grouping. This is large enough for stable estimation while remaining feasible with chunked CSV processing. The raw files will remain read-only, and all derived data will be generated by scripts with recorded filters and checks.

## 4. Unit of analysis and variables

### Unit and outcome

The primary unit is one team in one match (`matchId + groupId`). All members of a team share the same `winPlacePerc`, so player rows will not be treated as independent observations. The primary outcome is the team’s continuous final placement percentile. A Top-10 indicator will be reported only as a supplementary business measure.

### Behavior blocks

- **Combat:** kills, damage dealt, assists, headshot kills, kill streaks and defensible team aggregates.
- **Mobility and resources:** walking, riding and swimming distance, heals, boosts, weapons acquired and related aggregates.
- **Team coordination:** revives, DBNO-related measures, assists and other defensible coordination aggregates.

Team totals, means, maxima, dispersion and per-member normalized measures will be considered after EDA. Transformations will be selected using documented distribution and collinearity checks rather than favorable significance results.

### Controls and exclusions

`maxPlace` and `numGroups` may be used as match-structure controls. `killPlace`, `matchDuration`, `rankPoints`, `winPoints`, `killPoints`, identifiers, and any outcome-derived variables are excluded from the primary behavior model. A prior-rating sensitivity model, if used, will be labeled separately and will not be interpreted as a trainable behavior model.

## 5. Hypotheses

The hypotheses are fixed before EDA at the block level:

- **H1:** Combat variables provide incremental explanatory power beyond match context.
- **H2:** Mobility and resource variables provide incremental explanatory power beyond match context and combat.
- **H3:** Team-coordination variables provide incremental explanatory power beyond the preceding blocks.

The main tests are two-sided. We will report effect size, direction, 95% confidence interval, stability, and practical interpretation together. Nonlinearity and cross-mode differences are pre-declared extensions.

## 6. Analysis and validation plan

1. Perform descriptive analysis of distributions, missingness, outliers, zero inflation, team size, and within-match dependence.
2. Fit a context-only mean/baseline model.
3. Fit nested multiple linear-regression models by adding the three behavior blocks in the pre-specified order.
4. Use robust or cluster-aware standard errors, residual diagnostics, and joint block tests.
5. Hold out complete `matchId` groups for sample-out-of-match validation; never split player rows from one match across partitions.
6. Use match-level bootstrap or an equivalent cluster-aware procedure for stability.
7. Report RMSE, MAE, sample-out-of-match R2, block-level tests, standardized effects, confidence intervals, and sensitivity results.

## 7. Business evaluation

The final recommendation will rank training areas by:

- standardized association and incremental explanatory value;
- confidence interval width and stability under resampling;
- consistency across reasonable model specifications;
- controllability and interpretability for a coaching team.

The report will not translate coefficients into causal improvement, exact training-hour returns, profit, or ROI. The Top-10 supplementary analysis will help communicate practical differences without replacing the continuous outcome analysis.

## 8. Limitations and risk controls

- **Observational evidence:** associations may reflect confounding, reverse timing, or team skill; causal language is prohibited.
- **Post-match measurement:** the fields describe completed matches; the result is a training diagnostic, not live prediction.
- **Dependence:** team aggregation, match-level splitting, and cluster-aware uncertainty address pseudo-replication.
- **Sampling and version limits:** the Kaggle snapshot is not a random sample of all current PUBG matches; claims are limited to comparable settings.
- **Skew and outliers:** EDA, transformations, robust inference, and sensitivity checks will be documented.
- **Compute:** chunked processing and a narrow primary mode keep the workflow reproducible.

## 9. Go / Revise / Switch gate

The project proceeds to final recommendations only if data integrity passes, at least one behavior block has a stable practically meaningful association, the model improves on the training-mean baseline with a target of at least 5% RMSE improvement on complete-match holdout data, and the findings support a clear training-priority statement with uncertainty. If the gate fails after one evidence-based design revision, the team will re-evaluate the question or switch to Dota 2 or LoL rather than force a PUBG conclusion.
